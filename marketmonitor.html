How to read this

Primary trend is the count of stocks up 25% in a quarter versus down 25% in a quarter. More up than down is a bullish phase. That relationship flipping is the signal that matters most.

4% daily counts stocks moving more than 4% in a single day on rising volume. Under 300 is normal, 300–500 is high, 500–1000 is very high, and above 1000 is extreme — which usually means a climax rather than a beginning.

10-day ratio is ten days of 4% up divided by ten days of 4% down. Above 2 is a bullish thrust, below 0.5 is a bearish thrust.

Breadth divergence is the thing to watch. When the market keeps making highs but the breadth line rolls over, participation is narrowing and the move is running on fewer stocks.
